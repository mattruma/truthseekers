# Truth

**Truth is an accurate description of reality.** 

A claim is true when it corresponds to reality as it actually is, including any relevant conditions such as time, place, and perspective. 

Truth itself does not change, although the reality being described may change.

This definition separates two things that are easy to blur together:

- **Reality** is what actually is or was.
- **Truth** accurately describes that reality.

Our beliefs about reality can change. Our knowledge can improve. Circumstances can change. But those changes do not rewrite what was or is actually true.

## Time Does Not Change Truth

Suppose someone says:

> "The temperature outside was 40°F yesterday."

and later says:

> "The temperature outside is 70°F today."

There is no contradiction. 

Both statements can be true because they describe reality **at different times**.

The fact that it is 70°F today does not make the statement "It was 40°F yesterday" cease to be true.

A simpler historical example makes this even clearer:

> "John was 10 years old in 2010."
>
> "John is 26 years old in 2026."

John changed. **The truth did not.**

If the first statement was true, it remains true that John was 10 years old in 2010.

**A change in reality does not change the truth about what reality was at a particular time.**

## Place Does Not Change Truth

Location can work the same way.

Suppose:

> "It is raining in Detroit."
>
> "It is sunny in Phoenix."

Both can be true simultaneously.

There isn't one truth in Detroit and another truth in Phoenix. There are **two different facts about two different locations**.

The apparent disagreement disappears when we make the claims sufficiently precise:

> "It is raining **in Detroit**."
>
> "It is sunny **in Phoenix**."

Place can therefore be part of the context necessary to understand a claim.

## Perspective Does Not Necessarily Change Truth

Consider two people sitting across from one another with a cup between them.

From one person's position:

> "The handle is on the right side of the cup."

From the other person's position:

> "The handle is on the left side of the cup."

Both statements can be true because *right* and *left* are relational terms. They implicitly mean:

> "The handle is on **my** right."
>
> "The handle is on **my** left."

Their perspectives differ, but the underlying physical arrangement does not.

This is especially important because **perspective-dependent truth is not the same thing as relative truth**.

We shouldn't conclude:

> "It's true for one person that the handle is on the right, but true for another that it's on the left."

A more precise description is:

> **It is objectively true that the handle is to Person A's right and objectively true that it is to Person B's left.**

Perspective is actually **part of the reality being described**.

## Context Can Change Meaning Without Changing Truth

This gives us a useful general principle:

> **Context can determine what a statement means without determining what truth is.**

Consider:

> "The store is open."

That statement isn't necessarily subjective or relative. It may simply be **underspecified**.

- Which store?

- At what location?

- At what time?

Once we specify the claim:

> "The grocery store on Main Street was open at 10:00 AM on Monday."

we have something that can straightforwardly be compared with reality.

This suggests an important distinction:

> **An incomplete statement is not necessarily a relative truth. It may simply be missing relevant context.**

## Perspective Can Also Be Wrong

Two perspectives **can** both be true, but that doesn't mean they always are.

Imagine two witnesses watching a car accident.

One says:

> "The light was red."

The other says:

> "The light was green."

We shouldn't conclude:

> "They each have their own truth."

If they're talking about the same traffic light at the same moment and in the same respect, the claims genuinely conflict. At least one perception, memory, or statement is mistaken.

Contrast that with:

> "The car approached from my left."
>
> "The car approached from my right."

Those might both be true because the witnesses were standing in different locations.

This is why **perspective must be distinguished from truth**.

A perspective tells us **where someone is viewing reality from**. It does not guarantee that the person's understanding of reality is correct.

# A Simple Framework

**Truth is an accurate description of reality.**

A claim is true when it corresponds to reality as it actually is, including any relevant conditions such as time, place, and perspective.

**Truth itself does not change, although reality may change.**

- **Time:** Different statements can both be true when they describe reality at different times.
- **Place:** Different statements can both be true when they describe different locations.
- **Perspective:** Different statements can both be true when they describe the same reality from different reference points.
- **Context:** Context helps determine what a statement means. It does not determine whether the statement is true.
- **Contradiction:** Two genuinely contradictory claims cannot both be true when they concern the same thing, at the same time, in the same respect, and under the same relevant conditions.
- **Perspective ≠ Truth:** A person's perspective may explain why they believe something, but it does not make the belief true.

Reality may change, circumstances may differ, and perspectives may vary, but a true statement remains true about the reality, circumstances, and perspective it accurately describes.
