# Reality

Reality is everything that actually exists or occurs, independent of what anyone believes, thinks, perceives, or says about it.

**Simply put: Reality is the way things actually are.**

## Reality vs. Our Understanding of Reality

It is important to distinguish reality itself from what we believe about reality.

Imagine there are six apples in a basket.

- **Reality**: There are six apples in the basket.
- **Belief**: Someone believes there are five apples.
- **Claim**: Someone says, "There are six apples in the basket."
- **Truth**: The claim is true if there really are six apples.

A person's belief does not determine reality. Someone can sincerely believe there are five apples and still be wrong.

Reality does not depend upon whether we recognize it, understand it, agree with it, or even know about it.

## Does Reality Depend on Consensus?

Suppose everyone in a room believes a box is empty.

Someone opens the box and discovers a basketball inside.

Everyone agreed that the box was empty, but their agreement did not make it empty.

They were simply mistaken.

This illustrates an important principle:

> Reality is not determined by consensus.

Ten people can be wrong while one person is right. Likewise, everyone can be wrong about something.

What matters is not **how many people believe something**, but whether their belief accurately represents reality.

## Reality and Perspective
Our perspective affects how we experience reality, but it does not necessarily change reality itself.

Imagine two people sitting across from each other with a cup between them.

One person says:
> "The handle is on my right."

The other says:
> "The handle is on my left."

Both statements can be true.

They do not have separate realities. **They are describing the same reality from different reference points**.


Perspective is therefore part of the context needed to understand certain claims.
Different perspectives can reveal different aspects of the same reality.

However, perspective can also be mistaken.

If two witnesses looking at the same traffic light at the same moment say:
> "The light was red."

and
> "The light was green."

We should not automatically conclude that each person has "their own truth."

Their perceptions conflict. At least one may be mistaken.

**Perspective may explain why we believe something. It does not make the belief true.**

## Reality and Change

Reality can also change over time.

Suppose:
> "The temperature was 40°F yesterday."

and:
> "The temperature is 70°F today."

Both statements can be true.

The temperature changed. That does not mean truth changed.

If it really was 40°F yesterday, it remains true that it was 40°F yesterday.

**Reality may change over time without changing the truth about what reality was at a particular time.**

Time, place, and perspective can therefore be important parts of the reality being described.

## Is Reality Only Physical?

Our working definition deliberately does not say that reality consists only of physical things.

Reality might include things that are:

- physical or nonphysical;
- observable or unobservable;
- currently known or unknown.
- 
Whether nonphysical things such as minds, objective moral values, mathematical objects, or God exist is a **separate question to investigate**.

For example:
> If God exists, then God is part of reality.
> If God does not exist, then God is not part of reality.

Our definition of reality should not decide that question in advance.

**Defining reality tells us what we are looking for. It does not tell us beforehand what we will find.**

## Reality, Facts, and Truth

These concepts are closely related, but they are useful to distinguish.

- **Reality** — Everything that actually exists or occurs; simply put, the way things actually are.
- **Fact** — A particular feature or state of reality that is actually the case.
- **Truth** — An accurate description of reality; a claim is true when it corresponds to reality as it actually is.
- **Claim** — A statement asserting something about reality; a claim may be true or false.
- **Belief** — Something a person accepts as true; a belief may be true or false.
- **Opinion** — A judgment, evaluation, or preference about something; an opinion may contain factual claims that can be evaluated as true or false.
- **Perspective** — The point of view or reference point from which a person experiences or understands reality.
- **Perception** — How reality appears to us through our senses and experiences; perceptions can be accurate or mistaken.
- **Evidence** — Information that gives us reason to accept or reject a claim.
- **Knowledge** — Roughly, a true belief that a person has adequate reason or justification to accept.
  
## Putting It All Together

This gives us two foundational definitions:

**Reality** is the way things actually are.

**Truth** is an accurate description of reality.

Or, even more simply:

**Reality is what is. Truth accurately describes what is.**
